Privacy & Security

No. Everything you upload or type into a Rebrixe tool is processed entirely inside your browser. Your files never leave your device. They don't touch our servers. We don't see them. We couldn't, even if we wanted to — and we don't want to.

This isn't a promise dressed up as a feature. It's simply how the platform is built.

Yes — and this is actually one of the reasons client-side processing matters. When you process a file on Rebrixe, it stays on your machine. There's no upload, no server log, no cloud copy sitting somewhere with your quarterly report on it.

That said, we're a toolbox, not a compliance certification. If your organisation has specific data handling requirements, check with whoever handles that for you. But for everyday use? You're in a safe place here.

We use basic cookies — the boring kind that remember things like your theme preference. We also use advertising cookies through third-party networks (like Google Ads) that may serve ads based on your browsing history. That's standard for ad-supported sites and it's disclosed in our Privacy Policy.

We don't build profiles on you. We don't sell your data. We're not in that business.

Tools & Usage

Most of them, yes. Rebrixe is built to be responsive, and the majority of tools work fine on a phone. Some tools — particularly ones with complex inputs or side-by-side comparisons — are just more comfortable on a larger screen. We won't pretend otherwise.

If something feels awkward on mobile, a tablet or desktop will usually sort it out.

Since everything runs in your browser, the practical limit is your device's memory rather than a server cap we've set. For most tasks — image compression, PDF handling, text conversion — you won't run into any issues.

For unusually large files (think multi-gigabyte video files), a dedicated desktop application will serve you better. We're good, but we're not miracles.

Once a tool has loaded in your browser, many of them will continue to work even if your internet connection drops — because the processing is happening locally. You'd still need a connection to load the page in the first place, and a few tools that pull external data won't function offline.

But if your Wi-Fi cuts out mid-task? In most cases, you'll be fine.
