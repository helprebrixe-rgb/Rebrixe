SERP Simulator

Pixel-based Google preview — desktop, mobile & social. Keyword highlighting, snippet scoring, and live tips.

⚠️ How accurate is this, really? (worth a read before you trust the numbers)
  • Pixel widths are estimates. They're measured using your own browser's Arial rendering, so results can shift slightly by OS, browser, and device. This gets close to Google's real cutoff — it isn't guaranteed to be pixel-identical on every machine.
  • The 600px title / 920px description limits are widely-used reference points, not numbers Google has ever officially published. Google can change truncation behavior at any time without notice.
  • Mobile preview is a visual approximation — a smaller card and font, not a full simulation of how your specific phone would wrap and truncate the snippet.
  • Facebook and 𝕏/Twitter cards are simplified mockups. Those platforms crawl your page's actual og: / twitter: meta tags and cache the result — what's live may differ until you set those tags and the platform re-scrapes your URL.
  • "Snippet Quality Score" is a best-practices heuristic (length, pixel-fit, keyword presence). It is not Google's ranking algorithm and has no bearing on actual rankings.
  • Competitor Compare only checks title length and pixel-fit — not relevance, intent, or content quality. Treat it as a quick gut-check, not an audit.
  • There's no PNG export. "Print / Save as PDF" opens your browser's real print dialog. We won't fake a one-click image export — reliably rendering a custom favicon hosted on another domain onto a canvas can be blocked by browser security rules, and a broken export would be worse than an honest print button.
  • Nothing here is saved. Everything lives in this page only — refreshing clears all fields.

Why Pixel Width Beats Character Count

Google renders titles in pixels, not characters — "W" is roughly 3× wider than "l". This tool measures rendered width in your own browser, so you get a much closer estimate than a flat character cap.

The Title Sweet Spot

Aim for 50–60 characters and under ~600px. Titles in that range tend to display fully on desktop. Never pad just to hit a number — clarity beats length every time.

Keywords Get Bolded in Results

When a searcher's query matches words in your description, Google often bolds them — drawing the eye and helping CTR. Use the keyword highlighter above to see roughly how that looks before you publish.

Social Previews Are Often Ignored

Your og:title and og:description control how links look on Facebook and LinkedIn; twitter:title/description (or a fallback to og: tags) control X. A weak social preview costs clicks even on pages that rank well — set real meta tags, not just a page title.
