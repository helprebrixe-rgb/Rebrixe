What you need to know about print resolution

What is DPI?

DPI (Dots Per Inch) tells a printer how many ink dots to pack per inch. 300 DPI is the gold standard for photos — you can't see individual dots at normal viewing distance. Below 150 DPI images look noticeably soft or pixelated.

How many pixels do I need?

Multiply: width (in) × DPI = pixels needed. A 4×6" photo at 300 DPI needs at least 1200×1800 pixels. This calculator does it automatically for every common size.

Phone vs DSLR — does it matter?

Modern flagship phones (12+ MP) print sharp up to A4 easily. A 12 MP shot (~4000×3000 px) prints perfectly at 13×10 inches at 300 DPI. Cropped heavily? Resolution drops fast — check before printing.

What if my image is too small?

Two options: print smaller (check the safe size column), or upscale with an AI tool before printing. Upscaling blurry is still blurry — better to print smaller and sharp than big and soft.
